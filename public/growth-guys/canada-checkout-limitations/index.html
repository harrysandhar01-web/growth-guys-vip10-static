01

Location changes the calculation context

Province, postal code, address context, product availability, and delivery rules can affect what a storefront displays. A public non-purchase check can use a transparent test destination only to observe a merchant calculation; it cannot stand in for a reader’s real address or order.

For this reason, public verification records should label observed destination context and should not claim a single tax or shipping outcome for all Canadian readers.

02

Payment presentation can change

A merchant may expose different payment gateways by time, session, inventory condition, location, or operational status. A verified at checkout test should record the methods shown at that moment but must not imply that every gateway was available or tested.

No payment details are needed for a cart-code record. Stopping before payment protects the evidence boundary and prevents a non-purchase audit from becoming a transaction.

03

Use the cart as the final authority

This page does not make regional eligibility, tax, delivery, or payment claims. It explains why those fields can vary and why readers should review the current store interface before deciding what a code result means for their own cart.

This limitation is a feature of the evidence model, not a gap to paper over with stale claims.

04

A location is a test context, not a quotation

Where a public checkout asks for a country, province, city, or postal code to calculate a display, those fields establish only the context of that screen. They do not establish a customer identity, delivery promise, tax obligation, or final charge. The desk uses a transparently non-personal Canadian test context only when the public flow requires one to expose a cart-level shipping or tax display, and it stops before any payment step.

Regional results can still vary with stock location, merchant rules, session state, item mix, or later checkout information. A record should therefore name the field that was actually visible—such as a shipping method, amount, tax line, or “not displayed” state—and avoid translating that narrow display into a general claim about all Canadian orders.
