Overview

If a public report link cannot be fully inspected because a site blocks automated access, requires CAPTCHA solving, or fails to render, say clearly that you could not independently view the full report, describe exactly what you were able to observe, and stop short of inferring any missing values. Make an explicit access note, distinguish what you transcribed from what the merchant or site claims, and leave the laboratory result unconfirmed; also state that conclusions are limited to the specific sample, method, matrix, and date shown in the portions you did see.

Why: readers need to know what was actually checked and what remains unknown. Failure to separate observed text from vendor-provided claims risks overstating evidence. When a verification endpoint or report page resists inspection — for example an item on a public test site that requires interactive steps to view full data — transparency about what you could and could not confirm protects both accuracy and readers.

Quick checklist for publication accuracy

Evidence types at a glance | Evidence type | What to label it | |---|---| | Text you opened on the public report page | “Independently read” | | Values shown only on a vendor’s product page | “Merchant-transcribed” | | Content behind CAPTCHA or not rendering | “Not accessible / unconfirmed” |

Keep language precise and conservative: readers benefit more from a clear statement of what was and was not independently observed than from speculative interpretation. When access to a public report is blocked, accurate, cautious reporting helps readers judge credibility without overstating the available evidence .

  • Start with an explicit access note in one sentence: the URL reviewed, the date and time of your attempt, and the precise obstacle (CAPTCHA, “rendered blank,” timed out, or required authentication). For example: “I attempted to view the public report at https://verify.janoshik.com/tests/234011-Retatrutide_9YJ4G7CWMFZF on 25 Sep 2026; the page required an interactive CAPTCHA and did not render full report content.” This makes your limitation auditable and time-stamped.
  • State what you could independently read versus what was presented elsewhere by the merchant. If a merchant product page reproduces report values but the underlying public report could not be opened, label merchant-provided values as “merchant-transcribed” and any text you could open from the public endpoint as “independently read.” Example phrasing: “The Growth Guys product page lists a test identifier and potency figures; those figures are merchant-transcribed. I could independently read only the page header and test ID on the public report endpoint before the page required a CAPTCHA.”
  • Prohibit inferred fields. Do not guess missing entries, interpolate numeric values, or assume test methods or limits of detection from context you could not view. If the report’s method, units, or limits of detection are not visible, say so explicitly: “Method, units, and limits of detection were not visible on the blocked report and therefore are unconfirmed.”
  • Leave the result unconfirmed. Use language such as “unconfirmed by independent view” or “could not be independently verified.” Do not assert the sample passed or failed any criterion if the decisive data were inaccessible.
  • Add the required caveat about scope and limits: clarify that any visible values apply only to the specific sample, method, matrix, and date shown in the portions you saw; and note what cannot be proven by the report (sterility, safety, efficacy, regulatory authorization, chain of custody, or batch-wide uniformity).
  • Access note: “Attempted to open [URL] on [date/time]; page required interactive CAPTCHA and did not render full report content.”
  • Observed content: “Independently read: [list fields you actually saw, e.g., test ID, header, visible date]. Merchant-transcribed: [list fields seen only on the vendor product page].”
  • Unobserved/forbidden inferences: “Unavailable: full results table, test methods, units, limits of detection, and signature/certification pages. No values were inferred.”
  • Confirmation status and caveat: “Result status: unconfirmed by independent review. Any conclusions are specific to the visible sample, method, matrix, and date. No accessible material here proves sterility, safety, efficacy, Canadian authorization, chain of custody, or batch-wide uniformity.”
  • If a merchant product page lists a test identifier and numeric results but the public verifier page was blocked, present both items but keep labels separate. For example: “Growth Guys lists a ‘Retatrutide 30 mg’ product page that reproduces a test result and links to a janoshik.com test page ID; the janoshik public endpoint required CAPTCHA before full report content could be viewed. The numeric values on the merchant page are merchant-transcribed and were not independently verified from the public report” .
  • If you can open a report header or a screenshot but not the tables, transcribe only what is visible (test ID, date, specimen description). Do not extrapolate results or methods that appear later in the report.
  • It is reasonable to report identifiers and the fact of a linked public report even when the full content is blocked. Those facts are informative: they tell readers where the test purports to be recorded and whether a vendor is linking to a third-party report page. But make the viewing limitation front and center so readers do not take a partial view as full confirmation.
  • Include the full URL and the timestamp of your access attempt.
  • Explicitly state the obstacle (CAPTCHA, rendering failure, authentication).
  • List the exact text you independently read; label merchant-transcribed text separately.
  • Say which fields were not visible and that you did not infer values.
  • End with the unconfirmed status and the sample-method-matrix-date caveat, and repeat that no proof of sterility, safety, efficacy, Canadian authorization, chain of custody, or batch uniformity is established by the accessible content.
