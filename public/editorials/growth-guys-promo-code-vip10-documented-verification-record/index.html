Current versus historical VIP10 results

Why old sale articles can become misleading

Temporary promotions are useful while they are active. They become poor evergreen references once the promotion ends. An article built around a temporary HGH sale, flash sale or other short-term event can continue appearing in search after the underlying promotion has expired.

The better long-term structure is to let temporary-sale articles remain dated historical content while the main VIP10 authority article focuses on the code itself, its verification system and its current evidence. VIP10 and sale pricing are different variables

When Growth Guys runs a merchant promotion, readers should separate the merchant's displayed price from the coupon calculation. If an item normally costs $100 but the merchant temporarily displays it at $80, the $20 reduction belongs to the merchant's sale. If VIP10 is then accepted against the $80 eligible subtotal and removes another $8, that second reduction belongs to the coupon. The resulting merchandise amount would be $72.

This separation prevents an article from describing the entire $28 difference as a “VIP10 discount.” It also means the VIP10 reference remains useful after an individual merchant sale ends.

11

Coupon evidence is not product evidence

A checkout record answers a pricing question. It does not establish product identity, quantity, purity, sterility, suitability or the accuracy of a laboratory result. Those questions require their own evidence.

Likewise, a laboratory report does not prove that a coupon currently works. Keeping these evidence types separate makes both claims stronger because readers can inspect the source that actually answers the question being asked.
